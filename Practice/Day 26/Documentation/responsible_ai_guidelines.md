# Responsible AI Guidelines for Our Company

## Day 26 Internship – Mini Practical

### 1. Purpose

These guidelines define how our company should responsibly develop, deploy, and use Artificial Intelligence systems.

The objective is to ensure that AI is used in a way that is fair, safe, transparent, privacy-conscious, reliable, and accountable.

These guidelines apply to employees, developers, teams, and departments that develop or use AI-based systems for company activities.

---

## 2. Core Responsible AI Principles

Our company will follow the following principles:

### 2.1 Fairness

AI systems should be designed and evaluated to minimize unfair discrimination.

The company should:

* Test AI systems for bias.
* Use representative datasets.
* Review outcomes across relevant user groups.
* Investigate potential discriminatory behavior.

---

### 2.2 Privacy

Personal and confidential information must be protected.

Employees should:

* Avoid entering confidential company information into unauthorized AI tools.
* Avoid sharing unnecessary personal information with AI systems.
* Follow company data-protection policies.
* Use appropriate access controls.
* Protect customer and employee information.

Sensitive information may include:

* Passwords
* Financial information
* Government identification numbers
* Customer records
* Employee records
* Confidential business information

---

### 2.3 Accuracy and Reliability

AI-generated information should not automatically be considered correct.

Employees should:

* Verify important AI-generated information.
* Use trusted sources for factual information.
* Check AI-generated calculations and reports.
* Report repeated incorrect or misleading outputs.

AI systems should clearly acknowledge uncertainty where appropriate.

---

### 2.4 Hallucination Prevention

AI systems should be designed to reduce unsupported or fabricated information.

Recommended practices include:

* Using trusted knowledge sources.
* Using RAG where appropriate.
* Providing source references when possible.
* Performing factual verification.
* Human review for important outputs.

---

### 2.5 Human Oversight

AI should support human decision-making rather than replace responsible human judgment in high-impact situations.

Human review should be required for decisions involving:

* Recruitment
* Employee evaluation
* Termination
* Financial approvals
* Legal matters
* Healthcare-related decisions
* Other high-impact decisions

---

### 2.6 Transparency

The company should be transparent about the use of AI where appropriate.

AI systems should have documented information about:

* Their purpose
* Intended users
* Data sources
* Known limitations
* Major risks
* Responsible owners

Where appropriate, users should be informed when they are interacting with AI.

---

### 2.7 Accountability

Every important AI system should have a clearly identified owner or responsible team.

The responsible team should:

* Monitor system performance.
* Review incidents.
* Maintain documentation.
* Address reported problems.
* Conduct periodic risk assessments.

---

## 3. AI Development Guidelines

Before developing an AI system, teams should:

1. Clearly define the purpose of the system.
2. Identify potential risks.
3. Review the quality and suitability of training data.
4. Identify privacy and security requirements.
5. Determine whether human oversight is required.
6. Define evaluation criteria.

During development, teams should:

1. Test system accuracy.
2. Test for bias.
3. Test privacy and security controls.
4. Test hallucination and reliability risks.
5. Document limitations.
6. Record significant design decisions.

---

## 4. AI Deployment Guidelines

Before deployment, the responsible team should confirm that:

* Appropriate testing has been completed.
* Known risks have been documented.
* Sensitive data is adequately protected.
* Bias testing has been performed.
* Human oversight is available when required.
* Users understand important limitations.
* An incident reporting process exists.

High-risk AI systems should receive additional review before deployment.

---

## 5. Employee AI Usage Guidelines

Employees using AI tools should:

### Do

* Verify important information.
* Use approved AI tools.
* Protect confidential information.
* Review AI-generated content before sharing it.
* Report harmful or incorrect AI outputs.
* Follow company AI and data-security policies.

### Do Not

* Upload confidential information into unauthorized AI tools.
* Treat AI-generated information as automatically correct.
* Use AI to make discriminatory decisions.
* Allow AI to make high-impact decisions without required human review.
* Share sensitive customer or employee data unnecessarily.
* Use AI-generated content without appropriate verification.

---

## 6. Risk Classification

The company should classify AI applications according to risk.

| Risk Level | Example                          | Required Control                                   |
| ---------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Low        | General writing assistance       | Basic verification                                 |
| Medium     | Business analysis                | Human verification                                 |
| High       | Recruitment support              | Mandatory human review and fairness testing        |
| Critical   | Major healthcare/legal decisions | Qualified human decision-maker and enhanced review |

---

## 7. Monitoring and Auditing

AI systems should be monitored throughout their operational lifecycle.

Monitoring should include:

* Accuracy
* Bias
* Privacy incidents
* Hallucinations
* User feedback
* Security issues
* Unexpected behavior

Periodic audits should be conducted for important AI systems.

If serious risks are identified, the company should review, modify, restrict, or suspend the affected AI system.

---

## 8. Incident Reporting

Employees should report AI-related incidents such as:

* Biased decisions
* Privacy violations
* Incorrect information
* Harmful outputs
* Security problems
* Unexpected AI behavior

The responsible team should investigate the incident, document the findings, and take appropriate corrective action.

---

## 9. Responsible AI Approval Checklist

Before an AI system is deployed, the responsible team should confirm:

* [ ] Purpose and intended use are documented.
* [ ] Potential risks have been identified.
* [ ] Training and evaluation data have been reviewed.
* [ ] Bias testing has been performed.
* [ ] Privacy risks have been assessed.
* [ ] Accuracy and reliability have been tested.
* [ ] Human oversight requirements have been defined.
* [ ] System limitations have been documented.
* [ ] A responsible owner has been assigned.
* [ ] An incident reporting process is available.
* [ ] Continuous monitoring has been planned.

---

## 10. Governance

The company should maintain a Responsible AI governance process that includes:

* Clear AI ownership
* Risk assessment
* Model evaluation
* Documentation
* Periodic audits
* Incident management
* Employee awareness and training
* Continuous improvement

Responsible AI should be treated as an ongoing organizational responsibility rather than a one-time technical activity.

---

## 11. Final Guideline

Our company will use AI as a tool to support people while maintaining human responsibility for important decisions.

AI systems should be:

**Fair → Private → Reliable → Transparent → Accountable → Safe → Human-supervised**

The company will continuously evaluate AI systems and improve safeguards as new risks and technologies emerge.

---

## Conclusion

Responsible AI helps the company gain the benefits of artificial intelligence while reducing potential harm.

By following these guidelines, the company can promote ethical AI usage, protect sensitive information, reduce bias and hallucinations, maintain human oversight, and build greater trust among employees, customers, and other stakeholders.
